# Converting Arbitrum's USDC float into USDG

USDG is a **$3.05 billion** stablecoin whose supply lives almost entirely on surfaces its distribution partners control: **46% on OKX's X Layer, 23% on Robinhood Chain, 20% on Solana and 8% on Ethereum**, with roughly a third of the whole float parked in just two yield products (the Steakhouse-curated Morpho vault behind Robinhood Earn at ~$547M and Maple's syrupUSDG at ~$504M) ([DefiLlama](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoin/286); [DefiLlama yields](https://defillama.com/yields?token=USDG)). Four days after its 6 October 2026 native launch, Arbitrum One holds only **~29–31M USDG** across Morpho (Gauntlet and Steakhouse vaults), Fluid, GMX's new Dollar Vault and Uniswap ([Arbiscan](https://arbiscan.io/token/0x004B506865409877C9fA29bfb1ebA929984B9bbC); [Arbitrum blog](https://blog.arbitrum.io/usdg-is-live-on-arbitrum/)). The other major stablecoins are overwhelmingly Ethereum, Tron and exchange-chain assets; on Arbitrum the stablecoin base (~$3.8–4.1B, 7th among chains) is **56% native USDC ($2.67B on-chain), then USDT0 ($823M), PYUSD ($359M), USDai ($344M) and USDS ($100M)**, while RLUSD, USD1, FDUSD, AUSD, USDe and GHO are absent or dust ([DefiLlama](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoins?includePrices=true); [Stablecoin Beat](https://stablecoinbeat.com/networks/arbitrum/)). Segmenting Arbitrum's USDC shows that the headline $2.67B overstates the convertible pool: **$390M is locked in the Hyperliquid bridge** and contractually USDC-aligned, **~$330–410M sits in centralized-exchange wallets** (Binance alone $272.5M) that will only hold USDG once exchanges list Arbitrum USDG rails, **~$440M is in DeFi** (Aave $179M supplied, GMX pools $65M, Fluid $55M, Variational/Aster/Gains/Ostium perps ~$61M, DEX pools ~$60M) and is the yield-sensitive core, and ~$364M sits in 31 unlabeled top-50 addresses ([Arbiscan holders](https://arbiscan.io/token/0xaf88d065e77c8cC2239327C5EDb3A432268e5831#balances)). The decisive lesson from every stablecoin migration of 2023–26 is that **captive surfaces that default deposits into the coin, not DeFi listings or emissions, produced the nine-figure launches**: USDG itself sat at ~$0.3B for eight months until OKX and Robinhood made it their house dollar, and Hyperliquid's USDH died at ~$100M despite pledging 50% of reserve yield to the chain ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2025-07-14-okx-global-dollar-network-paxos-usdg-stablecoin-362514); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-05-14-coinbase-hyperliquid-official-deployer-usdc-401233)). The recommended sequence is therefore: convert Arbitrum's own treasuries first (~$66M ATMC plus $125M Foundation non-ARB assets), seed deep USDG/USDC protocol-owned liquidity, concentrate DRIP rewards on the venues that retained borrowing after Season 1 (Morpho 203%, Fluid 128%) with GMX's GLV vault as the retail funnel, pursue the **$359M of Paxos-issued PYUSD inside USDai** as the single largest same-issuer conversion, sign Arbitrum-USDG rails with the GDN exchanges (OKX, Gate, KuCoin, Gemini, Bitpanda, plus Kraken already live), and build deposit-edge auto-conversion (the Robinhood/Across pattern) via LI.FI and Stargate — all timed around the 15–22 October Snapshot and 29 October–12 November on-chain votes ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/adopting-usdg-as-a-core-strategic-initiative-for-the-arbitrumdao/31548)).

## USDG's $3.05B rides three partner chains and two yield products

USDG is natively issued on seven chains (Ethereum, Solana, Ink, X Layer, Robinhood Chain, Mantle and, since 6 October 2026, Arbitrum One), with a LayerZero-wrapped "USDG0" on Hyperliquid, Plume and Aptos that has attracted only ~$1.3M in eleven months ([Paxos docs](https://docs.paxos.com/stablecoin/usdg/mainnet); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/paxos-labs-and-layerzero-launch-usdg0-to-expand-global-dollar-across-defi)). Supply grew about **4.2x in twelve months**, from $719M on 1 October 2025 to a **$3.36B peak on 1 August 2026**, then contracted roughly 9% to $3.05B by 10 October, with the decline concentrated on X Layer and Ethereum and no public explanation from Paxos ([DefiLlama](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoin/286); [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/usdg-explained)). USDG is the seventh-largest stablecoin ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/paxos-3b-usdg-stablecoin-launches-arbitrum)).

The **Steakhouse USDG vault on Morpho (Robinhood Chain) holds ~$547M at 7.1% APY** and the syrupUSDG collateral market another $133M; **Maple's syrupUSDG on Ethereum holds ~$504M at 5.1%**; everything else is sub-$100M, including Aave V3 X Layer's PT-USDG market ($60M), Jupiter Lend on Solana ($60M), Aave V4 Ethereum (syrupUSDG $51M, USDG Core $10M), Curve USDG/USDC ($20M), Orca ($18M) and Kamino ($10.5M liquidity, $4.9M lend) ([DefiLlama yields](https://defillama.com/yields?token=USDG)). Both nine-figure products launched in July 2026, so Q3's supply growth on Robinhood Chain is essentially Earn deposits ([GDN](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/maple-finance-joins-global-dollar-network); [Morpho](https://morpho.org/blog/robinhood-chooses-morpho-to-power-new-earn-product)). On Aave Ethereum, USDG grew from $94M to $123M supplied in the 90 days to 8 October 2026, with ~82% utilisation and ~828 suppliers reported in June, after launch incentives of ~6.4% ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/direct-to-aip-asset-listing-usdg-arbitrum/25811); [GDN](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/aave-usdg)). Uniswap processed **$21.8B of USDG volume in September 2026, 98% of all USDG DEX activity**, which signals that USDG's on-chain life is swap-and-settle rather than long-dated LP positions ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/paxos-usdg-market-cap-3-billion/)).

Who actually holds USDG is best read from Ethereum, where labelled or plausibly custodial addresses control more than 60% of supply: an unlabeled address holds 101.9M (31.5%), Kraken Hot Wallet 3 holds 38.1M (11.8%), OKX wallets 32.6M, Paxos wallets 32.8M and Aave's Core Hub 10.2M ([Etherscan](https://etherscan.io/token/tokenholderchart/0xe343167631d89B6Ffc58B88d6b7fB0228795491D)). On Solana the top 10 accounts hold ~72% of 24,119 holders' balances ([SolanaCompass](https://solanacompass.com/tokens/2u1tszSeqZ3qBWF3uNGPFc8TzMk2tdiwknnRMWGWjGWH)). The distribution layer is three partners that each own a chain: OKX (X Layer, OKX Pay, OKX Card, margin and perps collateral, "hundreds of millions" held by its customers, and the new OKX Money app paying up to 10% on USDG in LatAm, Africa, South Asia and the Middle East), Robinhood (Earn at ~7%, Robinhood Chain as the only native stablecoin), and Kraken (up to 5% rewards, Krak app in 160+ countries, Ink, and now USDG deposits and withdrawals on Arbitrum One) ([GDN OKX](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/okx-usdg); [Blockhead](https://www.blockhead.co/2026/10/07/okx-launches-standalone-stablecoin-app-with-up-to-10-on-usdg-balances/); [GDN Robinhood](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/usdg-is-now-available-on-robinhood-chain-as-the-lending-asset-in-robinhood-s-new-earn-product); [GDN Kraken](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/kraken-usdg)). Beyond them, Mastercard added USDG to its on-chain settlement program on 3 June 2026 alongside USDC, PYUSD and RLUSD; Worldpay plans merchant settlement in USDG on Solana; Bitpanda brought MiCA USDG to 7M+ European users; and the GDN roster of 150+ partners includes Gate, Gemini, KuCoin, SwissBorg, Bullish, Anchorage, Wintermute, B2C2, Keyrock, Transak, Toku (payroll) and Alpaca (tokenized-equity settlement) ([Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/global/en/news-and-trends/press/2026/june/mastercard-expands-settlement-capabilities-to-include-stablecoin.html); [PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bitpanda-joins-global-dollar-network-bringing-mica-compliant-usdg-to-european-markets-302679601.html); [GDN](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/global-dollar-network-grows-to-more-than-100-partners-and-usdg-stablecoin-crosses-1-billion-in-market-cap)).

The economic engine is the Global Dollar Network's revenue share: partners earn on USDG they **hold** ("up to 100% of the returns generated by assets backing USDG held on your platform"), **mint** and **accept**, with the network distributing more than 90% of reserve earnings and payouts made weekly; no tier table, formula or percentages are public, and the Arbitrum proposal likewise discloses no rate ([globaldollar.com](https://www.globaldollar.com/); [GDN](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/global-dollar-network-grows-to-more-than-100-partners-and-usdg-stablecoin-crosses-1-billion-in-market-cap); [Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/adopting-usdg-as-a-core-strategic-initiative-for-the-arbitrumdao/31548)). This is the most aggressive open share among fiat-backed issuers: Coinbase's 10-K describes a closed Circle/Coinbase split in which Coinbase keeps platform-held income plus 50% of the residual, Tether shares nothing structurally, and chains like Arbitrum "receive no direct share" of USDC reserve income ([Coinbase 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978826000015/coin-20251231.htm); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/05/arbitrum-joins-paxos-led-stablecoin-group-global-dollar-to-capture-digital-dollar-growth)). Two caveats matter for planning. First, every chain with more than $50M of USDG has a partner-funded consumer yield program attached, and OKX's 10% and Robinhood's 7% headline rates exceed T-bill yields, which implies subsidy from marketing budgets on top of the reserve share; Crystal Intelligence characterises USDG growth as "supply that leaves when the incentive does" ([Crystal Intelligence](https://crystalintelligence.com/stablecoin/what-drove-the-stablecoin-supply-down/)). Second, USDG is issued by Paxos Digital Singapore (MAS) and Paxos Issuance Europe (FIN-FSA, MiCA), not by Paxos's OCC-chartered US trust; Paxos's GENIUS Act commentary names PYUSD but says nothing about USDG, whose US standing after the Act takes effect (by 18 January 2027) depends on Treasury's comparable-regime route ([Paxos](https://www.paxos.com/blog/genius-act); [Pharos](https://pharos.watch/stablecoin/usdg-paxos/)).

USDT ($184.2B) and USDC ($73.1B) dominate the market at roughly 2.5:1, but their geographies diverge: USDT sits ~95% on Tron ($93.4B, +$17B year on year), Ethereum ($73.0B) and BSC ($9.2B), while USDC is 62% Ethereum ($45.1B) with Hyperliquid now its second-largest chain at $7.1B natively minted, then Solana $6.8B, Base $4.4B, Arbitrum $2.4B, Polygon $1.6B, BSC $1.6B and Circle's own Arc at $0.5B three weeks after launch ([DefiLlama USDT](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoin/1); [DefiLlama USDC](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoin/2)). Three patterns in this table should shape the Arbitrum plan. First, the 2026 growth cohort is regulated fiat-backed coins with institutional or exchange distribution (USDG +147% YTD, RLUSD +91%, USDGO and OUSD from zero), while the shrinking cohort is yield-dependent synthetics and DeFi-native coins (USDe −24%, USDai −47%, PYUSD −20%, M0 −77%, and USR, USDX, deUSD and USDH effectively gone) ([DefiLlama](https://stablecoins.llama.fi/stablecoin/286); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/394582/resolvs-usr-stablecoin-depegs-after-attacker-mints-80-million-unbacked-tokens-extracts-roughly-25-million)). Second, holder concentration is extreme everywhere: USD1 ~87% at Binance, FDUSD 94% at Binance at peak, PYUSD-on-Arbitrum inside USDai, RLUSD in Aave, AUSD-on-Monad in Pendle, most USDS in Aave as sUSDS. Speed of adoption and concentration are the same phenomenon, and USDG's own X Layer concentration (~46%) is flagged by Pharos as a material risk ([Pharos](https://pharos.watch/stablecoin/usdg-paxos/)). Third, Arbitrum is the only general-purpose L2 where a fiat-backed stablecoin other than USDC/USDT (PYUSD at $359M) has real scale, and the absence of USD1, RLUSD and (until last week) USDG shows that fiat-backed issuers prioritised Ethereum, Solana, BSC/Tron and their own aligned chains over Arbitrum; the DAO's USDG proposal is explicitly a response to "competing chains attracting [stablecoins] with better distribution and stablecoin economics" ([Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/adopting-usdg-as-a-core-strategic-initiative-for-the-arbitrumdao/31548)).

Arbitrum's own stablecoin base is shrinking in the second half of 2026 while Hyperliquid, Base, Plasma, X Layer, Robinhood Chain, Monad and Tempo absorb new issuance. Excluding the Hyperliquid pass-through (which explains the ~2x gap between DefiLlama's ~$4B and Artemis's $7.7B May figure), Arbitrum's organic float has been roughly flat at $3.6–4.7B since August 2025, and it fell 8.96% in the single week to 16 September 2026 ([CEX.IO](https://blog.cex.io/ecosystem/q2-2026-stablecoin-report-35673); [Digg/crypto.news](https://digg.com/tech/24956e13-c2e5-4086-bd62-b6226dd241e9)). DeFi USDC on Arbitrum has roughly halved year on year (Aave Arbitrum TVL −55%, GMX −54%, Uniswap v4 −91%, Pendle −58%), and Aave's USDC reserve runs at ~89% utilisation, so the USDG programme is entering a market where idle lending liquidity is scarce ([DefiLlama aave-v3](https://api.llama.fi/protocol/aave-v3); [Aavescan](https://aavescan.com/arbitrum-v3/usdc)). Among non-USDC venues, Spark Savings ($307M), USD.AI ($346M of PYUSD), Pendle ($171M, mostly sUSDai) and Fluid ($139M lending plus $35M DEX) are where the alternative dollars live; Aave Arbitrum lists none of PYUSD, USDS, USDai or (yet) USDG ([DefiLlama protocols](https://api.llama.fi/protocols); [Aavescan](https://aavescan.com/arbitrum-v3)).

## Arbitrum's $2.67B of USDC splits into a locked bridge, exchange float, a $440M DeFi core and a $1.2B long tail

Native USDC on Arbitrum totals **2,668,168,442** at block 513,577,870 (10 October 2026), with a residual **$48M of bridged USDC.e** that is a wind-down asset, across **5.76M holder addresses** ([Arbiscan](https://arbiscan.io/token/0xaf88d065e77c8cC2239327C5EDb3A432268e5831); [Arbiscan USDC.e](https://arbiscan.io/token/0xff970a61a04b1ca14834a43f5de4533ebddb5cc8)). Concentration is extreme: the top 5 addresses hold 28.8%, the top 100 hold 53.8%, **2,208 "whale" addresses (0.04% of holders) control 81.4%**, and 5.40M "shrimp" addresses (93.8%) hold 0.07%, or about $1.9M combined ([Arbiscan holders](https://arbiscan.io/token/0xaf88d065e77c8cC2239327C5EDb3A432268e5831#balances)). The addressable base is therefore ~13,400 whale and shark addresses holding ~94% of supply, and within that the top ~100. The $1.23B outside the top 100 is dispersed across those mid-sized wallets and is reachable only through product defaults, not outreach.

## Captive surfaces and debt-side demand, not emissions, moved every stablecoin that migrated

USDG's own history is the clearest evidence that native issuance plus DeFi listings do not move supply. It launched in November 2024 with ~$285M, had Solana and Ink deployments by mid-2025, and was still only ~$357M in July 2025; it then grew tenfold only after OKX made it a reward-bearing default on X Layer (X Layer USDG went from ~$323M on 1 April 2026 to ~$1.72B by 30 June) and Robinhood made it the sole native stablecoin of Robinhood Chain, where USD deposits convert to USDG, Across bridges USDC from 13 chains and converts it to USDG on arrival, and Earn pays ~7% ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2025-07-14-okx-global-dollar-network-paxos-usdg-stablecoin-362514); [StablecoinInsider](https://stablecoininsider.org/usdg-q2-2026-report-supply-growth-partner-expansion-and-the-robinhood-chain-moment/); [GDN](https://globaldollar.com/newsroom/usdg-is-now-available-on-robinhood-chain-as-the-lending-asset-in-robinhood-s-new-earn-product)). Robinhood Chain went from $0 on 1 July 2026 to ~$212M in a week and ~$700M in three months. The counter-example is USDH on Hyperliquid: despite winning a validator vote, pledging 50% of reserve yield to the chain's Assistance Fund and launching with $15M pre-minted, it stalled near $100M against $5B of USDC within eight months and was sunset in May 2026 when Coinbase became the USDC treasury deployer; yield-share to the chain did not create user demand because the perps quote asset stayed USDC ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/339418/native-markets-secures-usdh-ticker-hyperliquid-governance-vote); [4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/usdh-the-path-to-10b); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-05-14-coinbase-hyperliquid-official-deployer-usdc-401233)). Arbitrum's launch recipe (Morpho curated vaults by Gauntlet and Steakhouse, Maple syrupUSDG, Kraken rails, plus GMX and Fluid) mirrors Robinhood Chain's; the missing piece is a captive retail funnel that defaults deposits into USDG.

Stablecoin-to-stablecoin migrations that completed quickly were all custodial switches where one venue controlled the balances. Binance ended BUSD support with zero-fee BUSD/FDUSD pairs and 1:1 conversion, taking FDUSD from ~$350M to ~$2.85B in about six months with 95% of supply held by Binance ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2023-08-31-binance-ends-busd-support-fdusd-248301); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/binance-fdusd-busd)). Binance, OKX and Coinbase auto-converted custodied DAI to USDS in March–May 2026 ([BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/03/dai-usds-migration-makerdao-sky-protocol-stablecoin-rebrand/); [MetaMask](https://metamask.io/news/dai-vs-usds)). Tether upgraded Arbitrum's USDT contract in place to USDT0 so that holders and Aave/GMX migrated automatically ([USDT0 blog](https://blog.usdt0.to/your-usdt0-now-on-arbitrum)). Open-market migrations (USDbC to native USDC on Base via a one-click swap, Sky's optional DAI converter) took months ([Circle](https://circle.com/blog/need-to-know-native-usdc-on-base)). For Arbitrum, the only custodial lever available is the GDN exchanges, above all Kraken, defaulting Arbitrum-network withdrawals to USDG and running zero-fee USDC↔USDG conversion; the on-chain analogue is the Robinhood/Across deposit-edge conversion, which LI.FI (already offering one-step swaps into USDG on Arbitrum) and Stargate can replicate ([Arbitrum blog](https://blog.arbitrum.io/usdg-is-live-on-arbitrum/)).

Arbitrum's own incentive record says what to fund and what to expect. STIP (~71M ARB, 2023–24) bought traders and activity reverted; LTIPP (30M ARB, mid-2024) found that stablecoin and LST deposits "largely stayed after the program ended, while much of the ETH left" ([StableLab](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/stablelab-analyzing-stip-s-efficacy-and-ltipp-comparison/24777); [OpenBlock](https://openblocklabs.com/research/arbitrum-ltipp-efficacy-analysis)). DRIP Season 1 (3 September 2025–18 February 2026) spent 16.7M ARB (~$4M at allocation) on lending-market looping and grew yield-bearing stablecoin supply from $130M to over $1B at ~$51 of market size per $1 of ARB, with the committee's own lesson that "debt liquidity is the most effective lever" and that incentives have the highest impact at zero-to-one ([DRIP February 2026 update](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/drip-february-2026-update/30628)). Retention, however, was protocol-specific: 180 days after the campaign, borrowing on Arbitrum stood at 203% of the pre-campaign baseline on Morpho and 128% on Fluid, but only 36% on Aave, 28% on Dolomite, 15% on Silo and 2% on Euler, and at the market level Morpho's DRIP markets kept just ~8–11% of their campaign-end uplift ([NikitaOnchain, Arbitrum forum](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/adopting-usdg-as-a-core-strategic-initiative-for-the-arbitrumdao/31548)). Unichain's $21.8M UNI campaign ended with TVL down ~86% from peak, and Plasma's stablecoin TVL fell ~68% within two months of launch ([Uniswap governance](https://gov.uniswap.org/t/governance-proposal-uniswap-unleashed-unichain-and-uniswap-v4-liquidity-incentives/25250); [Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/plasma-xpl-plummets-85-amid-declining-tvl-and-stablecoin-supply-38743)). At ~$0.18–0.24 per ARB, the proposed 100M ARB is worth roughly $18–24M, and the proposal's own target of 15–20% of ~$4B (i.e. $600–800M of USDG) implies $30–40 of USDG supply per $1 of ARB if fully spent, which is only plausible if retention is far better than Season 1's market-level baseline ([DRIP January 2026 update](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/drip-january-2026-update/30546)).

The precedents for adding or switching a base stablecoin on Arbitrum (GHO on Aave with a 750K ARB grant and a KPI of supply at least 10x the grant, USDe on GMX with ARB from GMX's STIP-Bridge allocation, USDT0 and syrupUSDC on Morpho/Fluid under DRIP) were all paired with ARB incentives, and USDG's launch follows the template with ">$10 million" of DRIP incentives already committed, the GMX boost and the pending 100M ARB ([Aave governance](https://governance.aave.com/t/arfc-launch-gho-on-base-set-aci-as-emissions-manager-for-rewards/19338); [GMX Substack](https://gmxio.substack.com/p/gmx-launches-an-eth-usd-wsteth-usde); [DRIP January 2026 update](https://forum.arbitrum.foundation/t/drip-january-2026-update/30546)). Regulation shapes who can be paid. The GENIUS Act bars issuer-paid yield but leaves third-party rewards contested, and MiCA bars interest-like rewards on EMTs, which is why Coinbase ended EEA USDC rewards and why GDN and Robinhood carve out EU restrictions ([CRS](https://www.legistorm.com/reports/view/crs/608234/The_Stablecoin_Yield_Debate.html); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/294033/coinbase-ends-usdc-rewards-in-europe-as-mica-deadline-looms)). The practical consequence is that the reward should flow to protocols, curators and the DAO as B2B revenue share (GDN "Hold/Accept" rewards paid to the venue, redistributed via Merkl) rather than as retail APY, which suits DRIP's design and sidesteps the geo-restrictions that already exclude US, UK, EEA and Singapore users from OKX's X Layer rewards ([OKX Learn](https://web3.okx.com/learn/usdg-xlayer)). Hyperliquid's own switch, in which it chose the stablecoin whose issuer shares reserve economics with the venue, is the same mechanism GDN offers, so the pitch to USDC-margined perp venues (Variational, Aster, Ostium, Gains) is a direct reserve-share deal rather than ARB emissions ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-05-14-coinbase-hyperliquid-official-deployer-usdc-401233)).

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question from "how do we convince USDC holders" to "which surfaces can default dollars into USDG." The convertible USDC on Arbitrum is not $2.67B but closer to $0.9–1.1B once the Hyperliquid bridge, Circle-aligned wallets and dust are removed, and the fastest-moving slices are the ones nobody has to persuade: the DAO's own ~$66M, the $359M of Paxos-issued PYUSD sitting in one protocol's reserve, GMX LPs already offered a boosted USDG vault, and exchange float that follows whichever network rails exist. Together those four slices can plausibly deliver the proposal's $600–800M year-one target without a single retail holder consciously choosing USDG over USDC, which is exactly how X Layer and Robinhood Chain got there.

The deeper implication is that Arbitrum is testing whether a DAO can play the role an exchange played for every prior stablecoin success. OKX, Robinhood and Binance could default users into a coin because they own the deposit edge; Arbitrum owns no deposit edge, so its substitutes are protocol-owned liquidity, deposit-edge routing through LI.FI/Stargate/Kraken, and B2B revenue share routed to curators and venues through Merkl. If those substitutes work, Arbitrum will have built the first chain-level stablecoin franchise that does not depend on a corporate parent, and the model will be copied by every L2 that currently earns nothing on its USDC. If they do not, the USDH precedent says the outcome is a ~$100M coin with a revenue line too small to matter, and the 100M ARB should be tapered rather than spent.
