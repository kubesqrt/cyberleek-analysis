# Stablecoin chain-bootstrapping and migration playbooks (state as of 10 Oct 2026)

Scope: how issuers/chains have launched and grown a stablecoin on a new chain, how stablecoin-to-stablecoin migrations were engineered, what the GDN/USDG model pays, what chains spent on stablecoin liquidity and what stuck, the practical lending/DEX listing checklist, and what Arbitrum offers an issuer today. Every data point is dated; pre-2026 sources are flagged "(older)". Secondary aggregators (stablecoininsider.org, eco.com, KuCoin/MEXC news, Spark) are flagged "(secondary)" and should be re-checked against DefiLlama/Paxos transparency pages before use in a plan.

## KQ1. Case studies: how specific stablecoins launched and grew on a new chain (date, incentives, trajectory, first integrators, failures)

### Takeaway
Every launch that reached >$500M on a new chain in 2025-26 combined (a) native issuance (not bridged), (b) a captive distribution surface that *defaults* users into the coin (an exchange or a chain that makes it the sole/default dollar: OKX X Layer, Robinhood Chain, Binance for FDUSD/USD1, Kamino for PYUSD), and (c) a yield pass-through or token subsidy. Launches that relied on DeFi integrations alone with no captive surface (USDH on Hyperliquid, AUSD on Injective, PYUSD on Arbitrum so far, USDT0 on Ink/Berachain) stalled at tens-to-low-hundreds of millions; and subsidy-driven supply (PYUSD, USDe, Unichain, Plasma) retraced 35-85% when the subsidy tapered.

### Inferences
- USDG's own history is the clearest evidence that native issuance + DeFi listings alone do not move supply: it sat at ~$0.3B for 8+ months (Nov 2024 - Jul 2025) with Solana and Ink live, then 10x'd only after OKX (X Layer) and Robinhood (Robinhood Chain) made it the *default dollar* on surfaces they control. For Arbitrum, the analogue is Robinhood Chain's Across "USDC in → USDG out" auto-conversion and Kraken as the on/off-ramp.
- Arbitrum's launch recipe mirrors Robinhood Chain's (Morpho curated vaults by Gauntlet/Steakhouse, Maple syrupUSDG, Kraken rails) plus GMX and Fluid; the missing piece versus X Layer/Robinhood is a captive retail funnel that defaults deposits into USDG.
- USDH is the cautionary case for "chain-aligned stablecoin with revenue share but no captive funnel": despite 50% reserve yield to the chain and validator endorsement, it stalled at ~$100M against $5B USDC within 8 months and was sunset. Yield-share to the *chain* did not translate into user demand because the quote asset for perps was still USDC.
- PYUSD-on-Solana and USDe show subsidy-driven supply retraces 35-65% when subsidies taper; GDN's structural (reserve-funded) rewards are more durable than token emissions but are rate-sensitive.

## KQ2. What the GDN rewards model pays partners, what partners must do, and how it compares with Circle/Coinbase, Tether, Ethena, Agora, M0

### Takeaway
GDN pays partners "up to 100%" of the reserve returns on USDG held on their platform plus extra for minting and accepting deposits; secondary sources quote ~97% of network economics distributed. This is the most aggressive open revenue share among fiat-backed issuers: Circle/Coinbase is a closed bilateral split (Coinbase keeps platform-held income plus 50% of the residual), Tether shares nothing structurally, Agora shares at issuer discretion with a loyalty program, and M0 lets each issuer program its own split.

## KQ3. How stablecoin-to-stablecoin migrations have been engineered

### Takeaway
The migrations that completed fast and fully were all *custodial switches* where a single venue controlled the balances (Binance BUSD→FDUSD, Binance/OKX/Coinbase DAI→USDS auto-conversions, Tether's in-place contract upgrade to USDT0, Aptos lzUSDC→native). Open-market migrations (USDbC→USDC on Base, Sky's optional DAI→USDS converter, USDH→USDC) rely on 1:1 converters plus fee-free pairs and take months; the strongest user-level lever is zero-fee trading pairs and a default-conversion at the deposit edge.

## KQ5. Practical integration checklist for a lending protocol (Aave/Morpho/Fluid/Euler) or DEX to list a new stablecoin - oracle, risk parameters, timelines

## KQ6. Why protocols and users choose one stablecoin over another on an L2

### Takeaway
Observable drivers in 2026: (1) who pays the yield - exchanges/DeFi pass through reserve economics only where the issuer shares them (GDN, Coinbase-USDC, Ethena, USDsui), (2) liquidity depth and CEX pairs (USDT for trading, USDC for custody/institutional rails), (3) regulatory status (GENIUS Act bans *issuer*-paid yield in the US but leaves third-party rewards contested; MiCA bans interest-like rewards on EMTs, which killed Coinbase EEA USDC rewards), and (4) default placement on the surface where deposits arrive (Robinhood Chain, X Layer, Binance).

### Cited Findings
- Dune (Jun 2026): USDT ~$190B vs USDC ~$72B; USDT is Tron/Ethereum-centric ($89.9B/$86.7B), USDC is Ethereum/Solana/HyperEVM/Base; "USDT for settlement, USDC for custody"; USDC captures institutional/regulated payment flows, USDT leads trading and retail remittance. — [Dune behavioral map, Jun 2026](https://dune.com/blog/behavioral-map-of-stablecoins); [The Block Learn](https://www.theblock.co/learn/408738/usdt-vs-usdc-comparing-the-two-largest-stablecoins)
