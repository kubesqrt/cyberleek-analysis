Method

The model is deliberately simple: revenue = visits × conversion × AOV. The work is in choosing the inputs from what a public visitor can observe. Each input gets a low, base and high value so the output is a range, not a point.

1. Collect signals

  • Platform and age. Shopify exposes /products.json and /collections.json; the earliest created_at dates the catalog. Policy page "last updated" dates and the Shopify shop ID also date the store. Domain registration (WHOIS) gives an upper bound.
  • Catalog. Product count, price range, variant counts, vendor field (a print-on-demand supplier such as ODMPOD or Printful means no inventory and a thin margin).
  • Proof of demand. Reviews app installed and the review count, sold-out variants, "X bought this" widgets, press mentions.
  • Marketing footprint. Meta, TikTok, Google, Pinterest and Snap pixels in the page source show paid acquisition. Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center show live ads. Klaviyo or similar means an email program. Social links in the footer and their follower counts.
  • Third-party traffic. Similarweb and Semrush report visits only above roughly 5,000 sessions a month. No data there is itself a signal.

Shopify store medians sit around 1.4%; the top quartile is above 3%. Mobile-heavy and cold-traffic mixes pull the rate down.

4. Derive the average order value

Start from the median catalog price. Multiply by items per order (apparel 1.2 to 1.5, consumables 1.5 to 2.5, single-hero-product stores about 1.1). Reduce by the typical discount depth if sitewide sales or welcome codes are visible. Free-shipping thresholds pull AOV toward the threshold.

5. Net it down and sanity-check

  • Apparel sees 10 to 25% returns and refunds; consumables and print-on-demand see 2 to 8%.
  • Gross margin: print-on-demand 35 to 55%, own-inventory apparel 55 to 70%, consumables 60 to 80%.
  • Compare the implied orders per day with stock signals and review velocity. A store "doing" 30 orders a day with zero reviews after six months is mis-estimated.
  • Where any cross-check above is available, calibrate to it and treat the traffic model as the secondary view.
